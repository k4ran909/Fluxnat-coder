DeepSeek has maintained a consistent strategy of releasing **open-weight models** under permissive licenses (typically MIT) since its rise to prominence. As of September 2026, the company continues to release both large-scale models and smaller, efficient variants[1][2][3][4].

### Key Details for 2025–2026
*   **Model Availability:** DeepSeek's core models—including the **V3** and **R1** series, as well as the newer **V4** architecture—are released with open weights[1][2][4]. You can download these checkpoints from platforms like Hugging Face to run them on your own hardware[5][4][6].
*   **"Small" Models & Distillation:** DeepSeek famously utilizes **knowledge distillation** to create smaller, highly efficient models by transferring reasoning capabilities from its large R1 models into more compact dense models[7][8][9].
    *   Examples include the **DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen** series (ranging from 1.5B to 70B parameters), which are designed to be lightweight and accessible for researchers and developers[9][10].
    *   The latest **DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash** (released September 2026) is currently marketed as the smallest model in their newest architecture family, featuring native multimodal capabilities and optimized for high throughput and fast inference[11][12][13].
*   **Coding Capabilities:** DeepSeek has a long history of specialized coding models, dating back to the original **DeepSeek Coder** (2023)[14][15]. The newer V3 and R1 architectures, as well as the V4-Flash models, incorporate advanced reasoning and tool-use capabilities that significantly outperform earlier dedicated coding models in tasks like Codeforces benchmarks and repository-level assistance[11][16][17][12].
*   **Open Source Status:** While the model weights and inference code are open, the original training datasets are generally not public[1][18][19]. However, community projects like **Open R1** have emerged to provide open-source implementations of the training pipelines (such as GRPO and SFT) to help the community replicate the performance of these models[18][19].
