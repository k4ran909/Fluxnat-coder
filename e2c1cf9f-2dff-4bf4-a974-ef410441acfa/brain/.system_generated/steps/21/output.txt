### 1. Fine-Tuned SLMs for Cybersecurity
Recent advances have made smaller models (typically 7B to 30B parameters) highly effective for cybersecurity, often outperforming general-purpose frontier models on specific tasks due to their ability to be fine-tuned for specialized, private, or local deployments.

*   **Qwen-3.5 (27B) & Gemma-4 (26B/31B) Variants:** These are frequently cited as the top base architectures for cybersecurity fine-tuning. Research indicates that fine-tuning these models (e.g., as seen in projects like *Novee*) allows them to achieve up to 80% of the exploit-rate performance of massive frontier models at a fraction of the cost.
*   **Cisco's Foundation-Sec-8B:** A notable example of a model further trained specifically on cybersecurity material (threat intelligence and vulnerability mapping), showing stronger performance on domain-specific tasks than its base architecture.
*   **Kimi K2.6 / K3:** Recognized in 2026 for high-end reasoning and coding capabilities, often used as the "brains" in automated security workflows.

### 2. Best Open-Source "Vulnerability Scanning" Agents
Rather than just "models," the industry is using **agentic tools** that leverage LLMs/SLMs to drive traditional scanners (like `nmap`, `Burp Suite`, or `sqlmap`) and interpret the results.

*   **OpenAnt:** An open-source, LLM-powered vulnerability discovery tool that goes beyond flagging by attempting to "attack" the code to verify findings, reducing false positives.
*   **Darkmoon:** A popular open-source autonomous AI pentest platform. It operates as an MCP (Model Context Protocol) host and can orchestrate over 80 security tools with a focus on Active Directory and Kubernetes.
*   **Garak:** The standard open-source "vulnerability scanner" specifically for LLMs. It probes models for weaknesses like prompt injection, jailbreaks, and data leakage, functioning similarly to how `nmap` functions for networks.
*   **Augustus:** A tool designed to run 210+ adversarial probes against LLMs to test the robustness of their safety training against real-world exploits.
